 A2 &middot; The chunking rule &mdash; why each H2 must stand alone 
 AI engines do not read a page top to bottom. They retrieve chunks &mdash; usually one heading plus the text under it &mdash; and cite that chunk in isolation. A section that begins &ldquo;As mentioned above, this also affects&hellip;&rdquo; is useless when lifted out, so it never gets cited. 
 
 Rule Why 
 Every H2 section makes sense read alone It will be read alone. No &ldquo;as we saw earlier&rdquo;, no &ldquo;this&rdquo; referring to the previous section. 
 Repeat the key noun in each section Write &ldquo;non-manifold edges&rdquo; again rather than &ldquo;they&rdquo; or &ldquo;the issue&rdquo;. Chunks lose their antecedents. 
 First sentence under each H2 answers that H2 Same answer-first rule as the intro, applied per section. 
 H2s are written as questions where natural Matches how the query arrives at the engine. 
 One idea per paragraph, 2&ndash;4 sentences Wall-of-text paragraphs get chunked badly and truncated mid-thought. 
 Numbers and tables over adjectives &ldquo;Reduced 36 holes to 4&rdquo; is citable. &ldquo;Significantly improved&rdquo; is not.

 6 &middot; H2: What is a non-manifold edge? 
 Body &mdash; ~150 words A non-manifold edge is an edge where the surface of a mesh connects in a way that could not exist as a real physical object. In a valid mesh every edge is shared by exactly two faces. A non-manifold edge is shared by three or more, or by only one. Three common shapes this takes in practice: a face left over inside a model after a boolean operation, two shells welded at a single shared edge, and a stray face with no neighbour on one side. Sculpting tools and text-to-3D generators produce all three routinely. Non-manifold geometry is not the same as a hole. A model can have zero holes and still be non-manifold, and a slicer will still refuse it. Related term: watertight mesh . 

 7 &middot; H2: Why do slicers reject non-manifold STL files? 
 Body &mdash; ~160 words A slicer works by intersecting your model with a stack of horizontal planes and deciding, for every point on each plane, whether that point is inside the solid or outside it. That decision requires an unambiguous surface. When an edge is shared by three faces, there is no single correct answer, so the slicer either guesses or refuses. What you see depends on which slicer you use. Cura reports the model as not manifold and may still slice it with distorted layers. PrusaSlicer flags &ldquo;object has errors&rdquo; and offers automatic repair. Bambu Studio raises a mesh error before printing. The dangerous case is the one that slices anyway. A model with non-manifold edges can produce clean-looking layers and then print with missing walls or filled internal cavities that waste hours of material before the fault is visible. 

 8 &middot; H2: How TEELI repairs non-manifold edges 
 Body &mdash; ~180 words + table The checker runs all six geometry checks on every upload, not just the non-manifold test. Repair then removes the offending faces, rebuilds the local surface, and re-runs the same checks to confirm the count actually fell. A regression gate sits inside the repair worker. If the number of holes after repair is higher than before, or the model is still not watertight, the job is marked failed rather than reported as done. Nothing is charged for a failed job. Example from a real user file:

│ Check │ Before │ After │

│ Non-manifold edges │ 47 │ 0 │

 9 &middot; H2: Can I fix non-manifold edges for free? 
 Body &mdash; ~190 words Yes, and for many files you should. Here are the options that genuinely work. Blender &mdash; open the file, enter Edit mode, then Select &rsaquo; All by Trait &rsaquo; Non Manifold. This highlights every offending edge so you can inspect and fix them by hand. Free, precise, and the right tool when you need to understand what is wrong rather than just clear it. PrusaSlicer and Bambu Studio &mdash; both offer one-click automatic repair and handle straightforward cases well. Neither tells you what it changed. Formware&rsquo;s online STL repair &mdash; free, browser-based, no install. Where these fall short is verification and volume. None of them reports a before-and-after count you can put in front of a client, and none handles a folder of thirty files. If you are fixing one model for yourself, use Blender. If you need proof the file is now printable, or you receive broken files every week, that is the gap this tool fills. 
 This section is not a mistake. Honest comparison pages get cited far more often than vendor pages that pretend no alternative exists &mdash; and answer engines cross-check claims across sources, so a page that omits the obvious free option reads as unreliable. It also pre-qualifies buyers: whoever keeps reading after this section has a real reason to pay. 

 10 &middot; H2: When automated repair is not enough 
 Body &mdash; ~110 words Automated repair is reliable on defects that are local &mdash; a handful of bad edges in an otherwise sound surface. It is unreliable in three situations. Meshes with heavy self-intersection, where several shells overlap, often need the intersecting geometry removed by hand before any repair will hold. Very thin walls can disappear entirely when the surface is rebuilt. And models where the defect is a modelling mistake rather than a geometry error &mdash; a wall below the printer&rsquo;s minimum thickness, for instance &mdash; are geometrically valid and will pass every check while still failing to print. The checker tells you which of these applies before you spend anything. 
 Why this section is mandatory on every page. Athena measured &ldquo;file repair quality&rdquo; as a negative brand trait in 56% of AI responses mentioning TEELI, against 3% for competitors. Stating the limits explicitly, in the product&rsquo;s own words, is the direct fix &mdash; it replaces the model&rsquo;s inferred doubt with a sourced, bounded claim.

 A6 &middot; FAQ format rules 
 
 Rule Detail 
 Exactly 5 questions Fewer is thin; more dilutes and pads the page. 
 40&ndash;60 words per answer Long enough to be complete, short enough to be lifted whole. 
 Answer first word is the answer &ldquo;No. Credits are held&hellip;&rdquo; not &ldquo;That&rsquo;s a great question&hellip;&rdquo; 
 Questions phrased as users type them Source them from Reddit threads and &ldquo;People also ask&rdquo;, not from imagination. 
 At least one uncomfortable question Pricing, failure, or limits. A five-question FAQ where all five are flattering reads as marketing and gets discounted. 
 No question repeats across pages verbatim Duplicate FAQ blocks across 48 URLs is the fastest route to a thin-content classification. 
 
 The five slots, in order: 
 
 Safety &mdash; will this damage or change my model? 
 Money &mdash; what happens if it fails? (answer: hold released, nothing charged) 
 Confusion &mdash; how is {this issue} different from {adjacent issue}? 
 Alternative &mdash; can my existing tool already do this? 
 Scope &mdash; formats, size limits, batch.

 A8 &middot; Uniqueness rules &mdash; the thin-content guardrail 
 Forty-eight pages from one template is programmatic SEO. Done well it is a durable asset. Done lazily it is a doorway-page penalty that takes the whole domain down with it. The line between the two is how much genuinely differs. 
 
 Element Must be unique? Note 
 H1 &#10003; Always &mdash; 
 Answer paragraph &#10003; Always Rewritten, not find-and-replaced 
 All five H2 bodies &#10003; Always Different examples, different slicer behaviour, different numbers 
 Before/after table &#10003; Always From a different real file per page 
 All five FAQ pairs &#10003; Always Slots may repeat; wording may not 
 Free-alternatives section &#10003; Always Blender steps differ per issue; slicer behaviour differs per format

 Widget &#10007; Shared Must be shared &mdash; standing rule 1

 Hard rule: no two tools pages may share a body paragraph verbatim. Before publishing a batch, diff every pair. If two pages are more than 40% identical by body text, one of them should not exist &mdash; merge them. 
 Sanity test. If a page would still be useful with the widget removed, it is a real page. If it would be an empty shell, it is a doorway page and Google will eventually treat it as one.

 B2 &middot; Worked example &mdash; /glossary/non-manifold-edge 

 H1 What is a non-manifold edge? 

 Definition &mdash; 52 words A non-manifold edge is an edge in a 3D mesh that is shared by three or more faces, or by only one face, instead of exactly two. Such an edge cannot exist on a real physical surface, which is why slicers and CAD software reject models that contain them. 

 H2: Why it matters for 3D printing &mdash; ~130 words Slicing software converts a model into printable layers by deciding, at every point, whether that point lies inside the solid or outside it. That decision only works if the surface is unambiguous. A non-manifold edge makes it ambiguous, because there is no single correct answer about which side is the interior. In practice this shows up in three ways: the slicer refuses the file outright, it repairs the file silently and changes geometry you did not approve, or &mdash; worst &mdash; it slices without complaint and produces a print with missing walls or unexpected solid interiors, wasting hours of material before the fault becomes visible. 

 H2: How to identify a non-manifold edge &mdash; ~140 words In Blender, open the file, switch to Edit mode, and use Select &rsaquo; All by Trait &rsaquo; Non Manifold. Every offending edge is highlighted, and the status bar reports the count. In MeshLab, the Compute Geometric Measures filter reports non-manifold edge and vertex counts directly. Most slicers will tell you something is wrong without naming it. PrusaSlicer shows &ldquo;object has errors&rdquo;, Cura reports the model as not manifold, and Bambu Studio raises a generic mesh error. None of them distinguishes a non-manifold edge from a boundary hole, which is why two very different problems often get reported with the same warning. 

 H2: How to fix a non-manifold edge &mdash; ~90 words For a single model, Blender is the precise option: select the non-manifold edges, inspect what is actually wrong, and delete or rebuild the offending faces by hand. Slicer auto-repair handles simple cases in one click but does not report what it changed. For a verified result with a before-and-after count, or for more files than you want to open individually, run the file through the free non-manifold STL checker &mdash; it reports the exact count before you decide whether to repair.
